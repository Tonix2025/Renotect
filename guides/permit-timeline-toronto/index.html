How long does a building permit take in Toronto?

Short answer: once a complete application for a house is submitted, the Ontario Building Code gives the City of Toronto 10 business days to review it. That is only one part of the timeline, though. Preparing the drawings, answering the examiner's comments and, in some cases, getting a minor variance from the Committee of Adjustment usually take longer than the review itself. A simple interior project can be ready in a few weeks; a project that needs a variance can take several months.

Here is what happens at each stage, and what you can do to keep the permit moving.

The City's review timeframes

The Building Code sets how long a municipality has to decide on a complete permit application. The timeframe depends on the type of building:

  • Houses: 10 business days
  • Small buildings: 15 business days
  • Large buildings: 20 business days
  • Complex buildings: 30 business days

Two words matter here. Complete means the application has every drawing, form and document the City asks for; the clock does not start until it does. And at the end of the review the City either issues the permit or sends a list of comments. Comments are common, and the revised drawings go back for another review.

The stages, from idea to permit

  • Measure and design. The existing house is measured and the design is worked out with you. For a renovation this can take a few weeks; for a new house or a large addition it usually takes longer, because the design has to be settled before the permit set is drawn.
  • Zoning check. For additions, new houses and anything that changes the outside of the house, the design is checked against the zoning by-law: height, setbacks, lot coverage and floor area. The City also offers a preliminary zoning review before you apply.
  • Permit drawings and engineering. The permit set is prepared and signed by a qualified designer. If there are new beams, a removed load-bearing wall or underpinning, a structural engineer designs those parts.
  • Application and intake. The application is submitted online through Toronto Building. Intake staff check that it is complete before the review starts.
  • Review and comments. The examiners review the drawings against the Building Code and zoning, within the timeframe above. If they send comments, the drawings are revised and resubmitted.
  • Permit issued. Once the drawings are approved and the fees are paid, the permit is issued and work can start.
  • Inspections. Building inspectors visit at set stages during construction, and the permit is closed after the final inspection.

When you need a minor variance

If the design does not meet the zoning by-law, for example a rear addition that goes deeper than allowed or a house that is slightly taller, it needs a minor variance from the Committee of Adjustment before the permit can be issued. This is the stage that adds the most time. The application is circulated, neighbours are notified, and the Committee decides at a public hearing. After the decision there is a 20-day appeal period before it becomes final.

Altogether a variance usually adds a few months to the project, more if the decision is appealed. That is why it pays to check zoning early: a small change to the design can sometimes avoid a variance altogether.

What slows a permit down

  • An incomplete application, so the review never starts
  • Drawings that do not answer the Building Code questions clearly, leading to rounds of comments
  • Zoning problems discovered after the drawings are finished
  • Missing structural details or engineering
  • Extra approvals, for example for trees protected by the City, properties near a ravine that are regulated by the conservation authority, or heritage properties

How to keep it moving

  • Check zoning before the design is final. It is much faster to adjust a design than to apply for a variance.
  • Bring the engineer in early when walls, beams or foundations change, so the structural details are in the first submission.
  • Submit a complete package. Every form, drawing and document the City lists for your type of project.
  • Answer comments quickly and completely. Each resubmission goes back into the review, so one thorough response is better than several partial ones.
  • Plan the start date around the permit, not the other way round. Booking trades before the permit is issued is a common source of stress.

The bottom line

The City's review of a complete house application takes 10 business days, but the full path to a permit depends on the design, the zoning and how complete the drawings are. Simple projects can be permitted in a few weeks; projects that need a minor variance take months. Checking zoning early and submitting a complete, clear set of drawings is the best way to keep the timeline short.

FAQ

Common questions

How long does Toronto take to review a building permit for a house?

The Ontario Building Code gives the City 10 business days to review a complete application for a house. If the examiner sends comments, the revised drawings are reviewed again.

Why is my permit taking longer than 10 business days?

The timeframe only starts once the application is complete, and it covers one review. Missing documents, rounds of comments, zoning issues and extra approvals all add time.

How long does a minor variance take in Toronto?

It usually adds a few months: the application is circulated, neighbours are notified, the Committee of Adjustment decides at a hearing, and there is a 20-day appeal period after the decision. An appeal adds more time.

Can I start work before the permit is issued?

No. Work that needs a permit should not start until the permit is issued, and inspections follow at set stages during construction.

How can I speed up my permit?

Check zoning before the design is final, include the structural engineering in the first submission, submit a complete package, and answer the examiner's comments quickly and completely.
