Design approach

A classic facade with a modern edge

The house is designed to sit comfortably on an established North York street while still reading as a new build. It uses a classic language, with a stone-look base, framed openings and a mansard roof, and keeps it modern through restraint: smooth cladding instead of busy detail, slim black window frames, flat black garage doors and a limited palette of light stone, dark slate and black.

The mansard roof does more than set the style. The third floor sits inside it, with dormer windows breaking through the roof line, so the house gets a full upper floor while the front elevation stays in proportion with the street. The windows line up from floor to floor, the entrance is marked by a projecting porch with stone steps, and the double garage is set into the same stone grid so it reads as part of the facade rather than an add-on.

Lighting was designed at the same time as the facade. Pot lights in the soffits wash down over the stone, and wall sconces between the windows and beside the garage doors frame the house after dark.

Custom homes in North York

Designing a new home in North York

Many North York lots are being rebuilt with new custom homes, and every design has to fit the City of Toronto zoning rules for the lot: building height, setbacks, lot coverage and floor area. A design that goes beyond them needs a minor variance from the Committee of Adjustment before a building permit can be issued.

Renotect designs the facade and interiors of new homes and prepares BCIN permit drawings for houses and small buildings, so the look you approve and the drawings that go to the City come from the same studio. If you are planning a new house or a major renovation in North York, a free consultation is the easiest place to start.

Process

How we design a new house

  • Site and zoning review. We start with the lot: its size, the existing houses on the street, and what the zoning by-law allows for height, setbacks and floor area.
  • Concept and style. We agree on the character of the house, from classic to modern, and the main materials, before any detailed drawing starts.
  • 3D design of the exterior. The facade is designed in 3D in daylight and at night, so you can see the proportions, the materials and the lighting before construction.
  • Drawings and permit. The approved design is developed into the drawings for the building permit, coordinated with the structural engineer where needed.
  • On site. During construction we answer the builder's questions and check that the facade is built the way it was designed.

FAQ

Custom home design in North York: common questions

Do I need an architect to design a new house in North York?

No. Houses are exempt from the requirement for an architect in Ontario, so a designer qualified with a BCIN in the House category can design the house and prepare the permit drawings. You can still choose to work with an architect.

What does the City of Toronto check before a new house is approved?

The building permit review checks the drawings against the Ontario Building Code and the zoning by-law for the lot, including height, setbacks, lot coverage and floor area. If the design goes beyond the zoning limits, it needs a minor variance from the Committee of Adjustment first.

Can you design only the facade of a new house?

Yes. Some clients come to us for the exterior only, with the facade, materials and lighting designed in 3D, while others want the full house, including interiors and permit drawings.

Can you redesign the facade of an existing house?

Yes. We design new fronts for existing houses as part of a renovation or addition, with new cladding, windows, porch and lighting, and prepare the permit drawings when the work needs a permit.

Which areas do you work in?

North York and the rest of Toronto, Vaughan, Richmond Hill, Etobicoke and the wider Greater Toronto Area.
